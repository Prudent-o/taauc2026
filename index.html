Overview

Recent workshop-attached challenge sites in top venues usually keep one public landing page with a stable structure and then expand each track into separate pages as soon as final task packages are ready. This site follows the same pattern.

What this challenge is for

We focus on agent systems that must act safely, follow rules, and remain useful in realistic settings. The goal is not just a single leaderboard score. The goal is to compare systems that matter in applied workflows.

What is already fixed

The workshop linkage, website structure, participation flow, speaker lineup, and organizer contacts can already be published. Track-specific rules, datasets, baselines, and metrics will be inserted later.

Why the site goes online now

The site gives teams a stable public entry point, even before every technical detail is final. It also makes later updates cleaner because the overall information architecture is already in place.

Track 2

Text Privacy Compliance Track

This track will study compliant handling of private information in text. It is intended for systems that must detect, transform, redact, or otherwise process sensitive textual content while preserving required utility.

  • Planned focus. Privacy-sensitive text processing, compliance-aware transformation, and policy-consistent handling of private content.
  • Likely submissions. Compliance pipelines, agent workflows, redaction or rewriting systems, validation modules, and evaluation methods.
  • Evaluation dimensions. Privacy protection, compliance quality, utility preservation, consistency, and robustness to hard or adversarial cases.
  • Release status. Label policy, annotation standard, and official scoring protocol are pending the final track proposal.

Participation

The site is prepared for challenge-only participation as well as challenge participation combined with a workshop paper or poster. The final track pages can add precise submission rules without restructuring this section.

Who can participate

Academic teams, students, industry groups, and independent researchers. Teams may join one track or both tracks.

What teams will submit

System outputs or executable runs, a short method description, and optional workshop materials if the team also joins the workshop program.

What will be provided

Track pages, rules, submission format, evaluation notes, timeline, FAQ, and a contact channel for clarifications.

Evaluation principles

  • Task quality. A system should solve the intended task rather than only optimize a narrow score.
  • Safety and compliance. A system should avoid unsafe or policy-violating behavior.
  • Utility retention. A system should remain useful after safety or compliance controls are applied.
  • Robustness. A system should handle hard cases, ambiguous cases, and realistic failure modes.
  • Clear reporting. Participants should make their system design and assumptions easy to understand.

Planned public materials

  • Task descriptions and official rules
  • Data access notes and licensing instructions
  • Starter kits and baseline packages
  • Evaluation scripts and scoring notes
  • Submission templates and FAQ updates
