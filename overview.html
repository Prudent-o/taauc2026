Overview

Why the public site goes online early

The challenge structure is already stable even though the two detailed track packages are still under preparation. Publishing the public pages now gives teams, speakers, and organizers one consistent place for updates.

Public Rationale

Recent workshop-attached challenge sites usually publish a stable landing structure first and expand the technical pages later. We follow the same pattern here because the workshop affiliation, challenge identity, and organizer team are already fixed.

What this challenge is for

The challenge studies trustworthy agent systems in ubiquitous computing. We are interested in systems that must act safely, follow rules, and remain useful when they interact with users, apps, tools, and context-rich environments.

The goal is not only to compare isolated model scores. The goal is to compare methods that matter in realistic workflows, including action quality, safety, compliance, and utility retention.

Why the structure is fixed now

The website architecture, workshop linkage, invited program, and organizer contacts do not depend on the last details of the track proposals. Those elements can be released now.

This lets us keep later technical updates clean. Each track page can be expanded without changing the public entry points that teams will already be using.

Already fixed

  • Challenge identity and workshop linkage.
  • Public page structure and navigation.
  • Program page with the draft workshop schedule.
  • Organizer and contact information.

Pending technical inserts

  • Task definitions and benchmark scope.
  • Official metrics and evaluation scripts.
  • Starter kits and baseline packages.
  • Final submission instructions and FAQ updates.

Design principle

  • Keep the public language clear and stable.
  • Give each major topic its own page.
  • Separate fixed information from pending details.
  • Make later updates easy to insert without redesign.
