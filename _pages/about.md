## Overview

<p class="section-lead">
This site is for the challenge program associated with our workshop on trustworthy agentic AI in ubiquitous computing. It is designed as a stable public landing page that can go online before every track document is complete. Once the two track teams finish their proposals, this site can be expanded with task pages, evaluation scripts, starter kits, and leaderboard links without changing the overall structure.
</p>

<div class="info-grid">
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>What this challenge is for</h3>
    <p>We focus on agent systems that must act safely, follow rules, and remain useful in realistic settings. The current challenge design emphasizes high-impact applied scenarios rather than static benchmark scores alone.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Current confirmed tracks</h3>
    <p>The challenge currently plans two tracks: one for medical agents and one for compliant handling of private information in text. Additional tracks may be considered later, but the public site is being built around these two confirmed directions.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>What is already fixed</h3>
    <p>The workshop linkage, website structure, participation flow, schedule style, and organizer contacts can already be published. Task rules, datasets, baselines, and exact metrics will be inserted as the track documents arrive.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Participation

<div class="info-grid">
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Who can participate</h3>
    <p>The challenge is intended for academic teams, students, industry groups, and independent researchers. Teams may join a single track or both tracks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>How teams can join</h3>
    <p>We plan to support challenge-only participation as well as challenge participation combined with a workshop paper or poster. Final instructions will be posted once the track packages are released.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>What teams should expect</h3>
    <p>The public materials will include track pages, rules, submission format, evaluation notes, timeline, FAQ, and a contact channel for clarifications.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Planned submission package

<ul class="compact-list">
  <li>System output files or executable runs, depending on the track design.</li>
  <li>A short method description for transparency and reproducibility.</li>
  <li>Optional workshop paper or poster materials, if the team also joins the workshop program.</li>
  <li>Team and affiliation information for announcements, certificates, and spotlight selection.</li>
</ul>

### Evaluation principles

<ul class="compact-list">
  <li><strong>Task quality.</strong> A system should solve the intended task rather than only optimize a narrow score.</li>
  <li><strong>Safety and compliance.</strong> A system should avoid unsafe or policy-violating behavior.</li>
  <li><strong>Utility retention.</strong> A system should remain useful after safety or compliance controls are applied.</li>
  <li><strong>Robustness.</strong> A system should handle hard cases, ambiguous cases, and realistic failure modes.</li>
  <li><strong>Clear reporting.</strong> Participants should make their system design and assumptions easy to understand.</li>
</ul>
